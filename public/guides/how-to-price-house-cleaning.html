How to Price House Cleaning Services

Price a clean by home size and type of service, then check the result against what each cleaner earns per hour. Here are the published rates, what moves a quote, and a worked example.

What moves the price

  • Type of clean. Standard, deep and move-out cleans take very different time. Angi and Housecall Pro both put deep cleaning at roughly 3 to 6 hours and move-out at 4 to 8 hours, so do not quote them at the same rate.
  • Home size and layout. Square footage, bedrooms and especially bathrooms drive the time. Housecall Pro suggests using square footage as a cross-check for homes over 3,000 sq ft.
  • Condition and pets. A first clean of a neglected home or one with heavy pet hair takes longer. Price the first visit higher, then drop to a lower recurring rate.
  • Frequency. Weekly and biweekly customers cost less per visit because each clean is lighter. Angi shows weekly visits at $75 to $200 and biweekly at $100 to $250.
  • Extras. Oven, refrigerator and inside windows are add-ons. Put each on its own line.
  • Crew size and supplies. A two-person crew halves the time on site, not the cost. Include supplies and travel in what you charge.

Check the price against your hourly pay

Whatever method you quote with, divide the job price by the cleaner-hours it takes. That number has to cover wages, supplies, travel and taxes and still leave profit. A small apartment still costs you travel and setup time. Set a minimum job charge from your hourly cost times the real time a small job takes, and show any adjustment as its own line.

A worked quote

One 1,800 sq ft home, standard clean, using example rates (yours will differ):

Standard clean: 1,800 sq ft × $0.10$180.00
Bathrooms: 2 × $10$20.00
Oven cleaning: 1 × $40$40.00
Total$240.00

Check it against the market: Angi shows a 2,000 sq ft home at $200 to $400 for a regular clean, so about $180 to $360 scaled to 1,800 sq ft, and $240 sits inside that range, although the $0.10 rate is at the low end of Angi's $0.10 to $0.20. Check it against your pay: at 350 sq ft per cleaner-hour the job takes 1,800 ÷ 350 = 5.14 cleaner-hours, so $240 ÷ 5.14 = $46.67 per cleaner-hour, inside the $25 to $70 range Angi lists before supplies, travel and taxes. A deep clean uses the 1.5× multiplier in the calculator (1,800 × $0.10 × 1.5 = $270 before extras). These figures are examples, not recommended prices.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting one price for a standard, deep and move-out clean.
  • Never timing a job, so you do not know what you earn per hour.
  • Giving away the oven, fridge or windows inside the base price.
  • Holding the discounted recurring rate for the very first clean.
  • Skipping a written estimate. Read how to write a contractor estimate for what to include.
