How to Price a Kitchen Remodel

A kitchen remodel is priced by scope first (minor, mid-range or major) and by line item second. Here are the published ranges, what moves a quote, and a worked example with markup.

Typical prices for a kitchen remodel

These are consumer-facing figures from 2026 cost guides, not rules. They show where the market sits so you can tell whether your price is far above or below it.

Sources disagree because they group costs differently: Angi and HomeAdvisor show labor as its own share, HomeGuide folds more into material lines, and a few aggregator sites quote Fixr's labor share at 50% to 60%. Treat the tables as a sanity check, then price from your own costs.

Price by scope, then by line item

  • Scope tiers. Minor (refacing, counters, a few appliances), mid-range (new cabinets, counters, sink, floor and appliances in the same layout) and major (walls or plumbing moved). Say which tier the quote covers.
  • Line items for everything that can change. Demolition, cabinets, counters, backsplash, flooring, plumbing, electrical, appliances, permit and disposal as separate lines make change orders easy to price.
  • Allowances for owner-picked materials. Cabinets, counters and appliances swing the price. Write an allowance per item and say what happens when the customer picks something dearer.
  • Lead times. Cabinets and appliances often have the longest lead times, so confirm delivery dates before demolition begins.
  • Layout changes. Moving a sink, range or gas line adds plumbing and electrical work. Quote it separately.

What moves the price

  • Cabinet type. Stock against custom is the biggest swing in the whole job.
  • Countertop material. From about $15 per square foot (laminate) to far more for premium stone.
  • Appliances. Single appliances run $350 to $8,000 each on Angi's numbers.
  • Moving plumbing, gas or walls. Turns a mid-range job into a major one.
  • Location. Labor rates and permit fees vary by market.

Check the price against your costs

Add subcontractor and paid labor, materials, dumpster, insurance, vehicle costs, software, unbillable estimating time and overhead, then add your margin. Common mistakes

  • Quoting one lump sum with no scope tier or allowances.
  • Leaving out the permit, dumpster, disposal or lead-time delays.
  • Pricing a small kitchen by a large-kitchen square-foot rate.
  • Taking a big deposit. Set a deposit and payment schedule instead.
  • Skipping a written estimate. Read how to write a contractor estimate for what to include.
