How to Price Handyman Services

Price handyman work from your own loaded hourly cost, set a minimum visit charge, then check the total against what the market charges. Here are the published rates, what moves a quote, and a worked example.

Typical prices for handyman work

These are consumer-facing figures from 2026 cost guides, not rules. They show where the market sits so you can tell whether your price is far above or below it.

Sources disagree on the minimum charge: Housecall Pro's price guide gives $125 to $200, while its price calculator page cites $75 to $150 and suggests 1.5 to 2 times your hourly rate. Thumbtack and Taskrabbit describe it as a minimum number of hours, commonly one to two. Check what competitors near you charge, because rates are higher in large metros.

Hourly or flat rate?

Thumbtack and Taskrabbit both say hourly pricing suits small or unpredictable repairs, while flat rates suit clearly defined jobs. Housecall Pro's calculator page recommends hourly for diagnostic and oddball jobs and flat prices for repeat work such as TV mounting or faucet swaps, where time and materials stay in a tight range. A flat price protects the customer from a long job, so build it from a realistic time estimate and your own rate, and add a line for anything outside the scope.

Build your hourly rate

Do not copy a range blindly. Housecall Pro's formula: monthly overhead ÷ monthly billable hours = overhead per hour. Its example is $2,400 of overhead over 120 billable hours, which is $20 per hour, so a $55 labor rate has to become at least $75 per hour before it covers business costs. Overhead includes admin time such as estimating, invoicing, messaging and supply runs. The hourly rate calculator does the same arithmetic with your own numbers, and the overhead rate guide explains where the inputs come from.

What moves the price

  • Who you are. Angi says independents generally charge less than companies or franchises, which carry more overhead and insurance. Housecall Pro lists experience and specialization as separate factors.
  • Job type. Thumbtack's averages rise from assembly to maintenance. Specialized tasks cost more per hour than general repair.
  • Bundling. Angi notes that combining several small tasks in one visit saves minimum fees and travel charges. For you it also raises the size of each visit.
  • Travel. Unpaid drive time quietly eats small jobs. Show a trip charge as its own line when the site is far away.
  • Materials and haul-away. Parts, disposal fees and supply runs are not labor. Thumbtack says debris and hazardous-material disposal can carry extra fees.
  • Location. Labor costs differ by city and region.

Set a minimum charge

A 20-minute fix still costs you the drive, set-up, clean-up and the slot you could not sell to someone else. Thumbtack's example: at $50 an hour with a two-hour minimum, a one-hour job is billed at $100. Pick a minimum that covers the smallest visit you will accept, state it on the estimate, and apply it to every job below that size.

A worked quote

One visit with three small tasks (mount a TV, assemble a bookshelf, re-hang a sticking door), using example rates (yours will differ):

Labor: 3 hours × $75$225.00
Materials: $40 cost + 30% markup ($40 × 1.30)$52.00
Trip charge$40.00
Total$317.00

Check it against the market: $317 sits inside Thumbtack's $134 to $460 range, and the $75 rate matches Housecall Pro's $55 wage plus $20 overhead example and sits near the top of its $50 to $80 independent range. The 30% markup is inside the 20% to 50% range Zarla reports from Housecall Pro. A single 45-minute task at the same rate would be $56.25 in labor, so a $150 minimum visit charge would apply instead. These figures are examples, not recommended prices.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing from the wage you pay yourself instead of wage plus overhead.
  • Skipping a minimum charge, so every short job loses money after travel.
  • Hiding materials inside the labor rate, which makes the quote hard to defend and impossible to adjust when parts cost more.
  • Quoting flat prices without a scope line, so extra work has no price attached. See how to price a change order.
  • Taking on licensed trade work as if it were a standard repair. Housecall Pro advises referring it out or partnering with a licensed pro.
  • Skipping a written estimate. Read how to write a contractor estimate for what to include.
