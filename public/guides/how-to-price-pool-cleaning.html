How to Price Pool Cleaning

Pool cleaning is usually sold as a flat monthly plan for weekly visits, with first cleans, green pool recovery and seasonal opening and closing priced as separate jobs. Here are the published rates, what moves a quote, and a worked example.

Three ways pool work is priced

  • Flat monthly plan. The standard for weekly service. Work out visits per month, on-site and drive time, chemicals and your target hourly rate, then round to a clean number. State whether chemicals, filter cleans and opening and closing are included.
  • Flat price per job. Best for first cleans, openings, closings, acid washes and green pool recovery, where the work is a defined job rather than a routine.
  • Per hour. Useful for repairs, extra work and anything you cannot scope in advance. Thumbtack reports a national average of $100 per hour for pool cleaners.

What moves the price

  • Pool size, type and condition. Larger pools, spas and water features take more time and chemicals, and green or neglected pools need recovery work first.
  • Visit frequency. Weekly service is the common choice for active pools. Biweekly and monthly service cost less per month but let debris and chemistry problems build up.
  • What the plan includes. Skimming, brushing, vacuuming and emptying baskets are the base. Chemicals, filter cleaning, equipment checks and algae treatment are often extra.
  • Cover type. HomeGuide says closing a pool with no cover averages $365, a mesh cover $420 and a solid cover with bags $520.
  • Route density. Many pools on one street cost less per stop than the same number scattered across town.
  • Market. Year-round markets bill 12 months. Northern pools bill a shorter season plus opening and closing.

Check the price against your costs

Estimate on-site time plus drive time for each visit, multiply by your hourly cost, then add chemicals, fuel, equipment wear, insurance and overhead. See how to calculate labor cost and your overhead rate, and use the hourly rate calculator to find your floor.

A worked quote

A weekly service plan for one residential pool, using example rates (yours will differ). Each visit takes 30 minutes on site plus 10 minutes of driving, so 4 visits a month is 4 × 40 = 160 minutes, or 2.67 hours. At an example labor cost of $45 per hour, 2.67 × $45 = $120.00. Add a $20 chemicals allowance for a monthly price of $140.00.

These figures are examples, not recommended prices.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing a green or neglected pool at the routine rate instead of quoting recovery separately.
  • Not saying whether chemicals, filter cleans and opening and closing are included.
  • Forgetting drive time and chemical costs when setting a monthly price.
  • Skipping a written estimate. Read how to write a contractor estimate for what to include.
