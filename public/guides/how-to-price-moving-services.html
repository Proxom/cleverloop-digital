How to Price Moving Services

Local movers charge by the hour per mover with a minimum of several hours, and long-distance movers charge by weight or a flat rate. Packing, stairs, long carries and specialty items are extra. Here are the published rates, what moves a quote, and a worked example.

Compare several sources and your local competitors before you set a rate.

Hourly, flat rate, or by weight

  • Hourly per mover. The usual way to price a local move. Total = movers × hourly rate × hours, with a minimum. Say whether the truck, fuel and travel time are included.
  • Flat rate. Moving.com describes it as your estimated hours multiplied by the hourly rate, often with add-ons included. It protects the customer from a slow day, so it only works if your hour estimate is good.
  • By weight and distance. Long-distance moves are priced per pound plus mileage and valuation coverage. Thumbtack notes that weight and distance, not hours, drive these quotes.

What moves the price

  • Crew size and hours. Two movers cost twice one, so team size is the main thing to watch.
  • Home size. Angi's Los Angeles table runs from $295 – $827 for one bedroom to $3,543 – $4,724 for five.
  • Access. Moving.com lists long carry fees when the truck cannot park close and stair carry fees for several flights.
  • Packing. Angi says packing and unpacking are not automatically included in local moves.
  • Specialty items. HomeGuide lists items such as a pool table as a reason for a higher hourly rate.
  • Season and demand. Moving.com says rates change with the time of year, and HomeGuide says off-season (late fall to early spring) is cheapest.

Check the price against your costs

Add paid labor for every mover including loading, unloading and drive time, plus truck cost, fuel, insurance, equipment wear and overhead. Divide by the crew hours you can really bill. See how to calculate labor cost and your overhead rate, and use the hourly rate calculator to find your floor.

A worked quote

A two-bedroom local move with two movers and a truck, using example rates (yours will differ). The crew rate is 2 × $75 = $150.00 per hour, for 4 hours: 4 × $150 = $600.00. Disassembling and reassembling beds and a table takes half an hour of one mover at $100 per hour, which is $50.00. The customer buys 20 boxes at $3, which is $60.00.

Crew, 2 movers × $75 × 4 hours$600.00
Furniture disassembly, 0.5 hour × $100$50.00
Packing boxes, 20 × $3$60.00
Total$710.00

Check it against the market: $75 per mover per hour sits inside HomeGuide's $50 to $100+ range and near Angi's $80 average, 4 hours is inside the common 3 to 5 hour minimum, $100 per hour is inside Angi's $50 to $150 disassembly range, $3 per box is inside the $2 to $20 supplies range, and $710 is inside the $300 to $1,500 and $900 to $2,800 local totals. These figures are examples, not recommended prices.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping a minimum, so a two-hour job loses money after drive and loading time.
  • Not saying whether truck, fuel and travel time are in the hourly rate.
  • Leaving stairs, long carries, packing and disassembly off the quote, then arguing about them on moving day.
  • Skipping a written estimate. Read how to write a contractor estimate for what to include.
