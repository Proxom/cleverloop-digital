How to Find Your Contractor Break-Even Point

Busy is not the same as profitable. Your break-even point tells you how many jobs a month it takes just to pay the bills that arrive whether you work or not. Every job past that number is where profit starts.

Step 1: Add up monthly fixed overhead

Fixed overhead is what you pay even in a month with no jobs: truck payments, insurance, phone, software, office or yard rent, your own base pay if you take a salary. Use last year's totals divided by 12. Example: $4,000 per month. If you have not worked this out yet, see how to calculate contractor overhead.

Step 2: Find the contribution per job

Contribution is what a typical job leaves after its own direct costs (materials, true labor cost, permits, dump fees, subcontractors):

Average job price$900
Direct cost per job$500
Contribution per job$400

Step 3: Divide

Break-even jobs: $4,000 ÷ $40010 jobs per month
Contribution margin: $400 ÷ $90044.4%
Break-even revenue: 10 × $900$9,000 per month

What the number tells you

Only 7 jobs this month: 7 × $400 − $4,000−$1,200 (loss)
Jobs needed for $2,000 profit: ($4,000 + $2,000) ÷ $40015 jobs
Raise the average price to $1,000: $4,000 ÷ $5008 jobs

A $100 higher average price cuts break-even from 10 jobs to 8, the same effect as winning two extra jobs every month. That is why pricing from a target margin rather than a markup matters.
