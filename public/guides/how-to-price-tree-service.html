How to Price Tree Service

Tree work is usually priced per tree, set by height and access, with a minimum job fee, stump grinding by the inch and cleanup as extra lines. Here are the published rates, what moves a quote, and a worked example.

Typical prices for tree service

These are consumer-facing figures from 2026 cost guides, not rules. They show where the market sits so you can tell whether your price is far above or below it.

The sources disagree, partly because they count different things: some include cleanup and some do not, and the cost of removal changes sharply with height and what stands under the tree. Compare several sources and your local competitors before you set a rate.

Per tree, per hour, or by the day

  • Flat price per tree. The usual method for trimming and removal. Price it from height, spread, condition and what is underneath, then name what is included: cleanup, chipping, wood left on site.
  • Hourly crew rate. Fits storm cleanup and jobs where the scope is unclear. Thumbtack lists an average of $60 to $80 per hour for trimming, with emergency work much higher.
  • Per inch of diameter. The normal method for stump grinding, with a minimum fee so that small stumps still pay for the trip.
  • Day rate for equipment. A crane or lift is a separate line, since Angi lists $250 to $600 per day.

What moves the price

  • Height and trunk diameter. Small ornamentals and tall hardwoods are different jobs.
  • Access and targets. Fences, roofs, power lines and tight yards mean rigging, lowering pieces and more labor.
  • Condition. Dead, damaged or diseased trees have to be taken down in sections, which raises the price.
  • Cleanup. Chipping, hauling brush and logs, and grinding or leaving the stump are separate lines.
  • Emergency and storm work. Charge an emergency rate and say so before work starts.
  • Permits and heritage trees. Some towns require a permit. Check local rules.

Check the price against your costs

Add paid labor for the whole crew, the truck and chipper, fuel, insurance (tree work is high risk), software, the unbillable hours and overhead, then divide by the hours you can really bill. A worked quote

Removing one medium tree and grinding its stump, using example rates (yours will differ). The takedown is priced at $900 flat. The stump is 30 inches across and ground at $4 per inch: 30 × $4 = $120.00, which clears a typical $100 minimum. Hauling the brush and logs is $150.

Tree removal, sectional takedown$900.00
Stump grinding, 30 in. × $4$120.00
Haul-away of brush and logs$150.00
Total$1,170.00

Check it against the market: $900 for removal sits inside HomeGuide's $400 to $1,200 and Thumbtack's $450 to $1,450 ranges, $4 per inch is inside Angi's $2 to $5, $120 is inside Angi's $120 to $400 for most stumps, $150 hauling is inside HomeGuide's $75 to $250, and $1,170 is inside Thumbtack's $884 to $1,627 for removing a tree and its stump. These figures are examples, not recommended prices.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting from the ground without checking what the tree overhangs, then eating the cost of rigging.
  • Not stating whether cleanup, wood and the stump are included.
  • Forgetting the minimum job fee, so a small trim loses money on the drive alone.
  • Skipping a written estimate. Read how to write a contractor estimate for what to include.
