How to Price Drywall Installation

Price drywall per square foot of finished surface, with finish level, texture, repairs and removal as separate lines and waste added to the sheets. Here are the published rates, what moves a quote, and a worked example.

Typical installed price for drywall

These are consumer-facing figures from Angi's 2026 cost guides, not rules. They show where the market sits so you can tell whether your price is far above or below it.

Angi's per-panel prices in 2026 run from about $15 for 1/4 inch to $23 for 3/4 inch, with standard 1/2 inch wall board at about $20 and 5/8 inch ceiling board at about $21. Check your local supplier; sheet prices vary by region and size.

What moves the price

  • Finish level. A basic hang is a fraction of a Level 4 paint-ready wall or a Level 5 skim coat. Write the finish level into the estimate so the customer knows what they are buying.
  • Texture. Matching or applying texture is its own task at $1 to $1.95 per sq ft on Angi's figures. Price it as a separate line.
  • Ceilings. Ceilings are slower and heavier than walls, and 5/8 inch board is common on them. If your rate differs, add a ceiling line rather than averaging.
  • Tear-out. Paneling, plaster and popcorn ceilings all cost extra to remove. Do not fold removal into the hang rate.
  • Job size. Small jobs carry a higher rate per square foot because setup, travel and cleanup cost roughly the same whatever the size.

Calculate sheets with waste first

Wall area is the room perimeter times the ceiling height, and ceiling area is length times width. Add them, deduct doors and windows, add a waste allowance, then divide by the sheet area and round up. A 14×12 ft room with 8 ft walls and a ceiling is 584 sq ft; after 40 sq ft of openings that is 544 sq ft net, and with 10 percent waste you order 19 sheets of 4×8. The drywall estimate calculator does the area, waste and rounding for you.

Set a minimum charge

A single patch can cost as much to reach as a bigger job. Set a minimum job charge from your crew's hourly cost times the real time a small job takes, and batch several repairs into one visit where you can. See how to calculate labor cost and your overhead rate for the inputs.

A worked quote

One 14×12 ft room with 8 ft walls and a ceiling, using example rates (yours will differ):

Drywall: 19 sheets (544 sq ft net + 10% waste) × $14$266.00
Hang and finish: 544 sq ft × $2.25$1,224.00
Total$1,490.00

Check it against the market: $1,490 over 544 sq ft is about $2.74 per sq ft installed, inside Angi's $1.50 to $3 overall range. The $2.25 hang-and-finish labor rate is above Angi's $1 to $2.10 labor-only range because it bundles finishing and covers your overhead and profit; confirm yours with the job price calculator. The $14 sheet price is an example below Angi's $20 average, so use your supplier's price. Texture, patches and haul-away are not included; add them as separate lines. These figures are examples, not recommended prices.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting a single square-foot number without saying the finish level.
  • Folding texture, patches and tear-out into the main rate, so the customer cannot compare bids line by line.
  • Charging only for the net area and forgetting to charge for the waste sheets.
  • Pricing small repairs per square foot, with no minimum for the trip.
  • Taking no deposit. Read how to write a contractor estimate for deposit wording.
