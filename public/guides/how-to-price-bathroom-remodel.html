How to Price a Bathroom Remodel

A bathroom remodel is priced by scope first (cosmetic, partial or full) and by line item second. Here are the published ranges, what moves a quote, and a worked example with markup.

Typical prices for a bathroom remodel

These are consumer-facing figures from 2026 cost guides, not rules. They show where the market sits so you can tell whether your price is far above or below it.

Sources disagree because they define the job differently: some include demolition, permits and contractor markup, some list labor and materials separately, and Angi's own pages show both a $70 to $250 per square foot range and a $12,000 average. Treat the tables as a sanity check, then price from your own costs.

Price by scope, then by line item

  • Scope tiers. Cosmetic (paint, fixtures, vanity), partial (new tub or shower, tile, vanity, toilet, plumbing stays) and full (gut, layout change). Say which tier the quote is for.
  • Line items for everything that can change. Demolition, plumbing, electrical, tile, vanity, fixtures, glass, permit and disposal as separate lines make change orders easy to price.
  • Allowances for owner-picked materials. Tile, vanity and fixtures swing the price. Write an allowance per item and say what happens when the customer picks something dearer.
  • Contingency. Demolition can uncover rot or old plumbing. HomeGuide suggests a 10% to 20% contingency.
  • Small bathrooms are not cheap per foot. Many trades are needed regardless of size, so labor is a bigger share and the per-square-foot rate is higher.

What moves the price

  • Moving plumbing. Relocating a toilet, tub or shower drain is the biggest price jump (Block: rough-in $3,000 to $8,000).
  • Shower type. A prefab kit starts near $600; a custom tile shower with glass is many times that.
  • Tile choice and pattern. Installed tile runs $10 to $50 per square foot on Angi's numbers; large-format and natural stone take more labor.
  • Age of the house. Block notes plaster walls and cast-iron drains can raise demolition costs.
  • Location. Labor rates and permit fees vary by market; New York mid-range work commonly reaches $25,000 (Block).

Check the price against your costs

Add subcontractor and paid labor, materials, dumpster, insurance, vehicle costs, software, unbillable estimating time and overhead, then add your margin. A worked quote

A partial remodel of a 40 sq ft full bath where the plumbing stays in place, using example prices (yours will differ). Each line is a separate item, and a 15% contractor markup is added to the subtotal.

Demolition and disposal$1,500.00
Vanity with top, installed$1,200.00
Toilet, installed$400.00
Faucet and fixtures, installed$450.00
Floor tile, 40 sq ft × $15$600.00
Shower wall tile, 50 sq ft × $20$1,000.00
Glass shower door$900.00
Permit$400.00
Subtotal$6,450.00
Contractor markup, 15% of $6,450.00$967.50
Total$7,417.50

Check it against the market: $7,417.50 over 40 sq ft is about $185 per square foot, inside HomeGuide's $150 to $300 range for partial remodels and inside HomeAdvisor's $6,651 to $17,659 typical total. It is below Thumbtack's $9,809 to $18,640 full-bath range because the plumbing does not move. Demolition $1,500 is inside $600 to $2,000 (HomeGuide), the vanity inside $500 to $3,600, the toilet inside $120 to $800, the faucet inside $200 to $600, shower tile at $20 inside Angi's $10 to $50, the glass door inside $400 to $1,900, the permit inside $200 to $800, and the 15% markup inside 10% to 20%. These figures are examples, not recommended prices.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting one lump sum with no scope tier or allowances.
  • Leaving out the permit, dumpster or contingency.
  • Pricing a small bath by the large-bath square-foot rate.
  • Taking a big deposit. HomeGuide advises keeping it small; set a deposit and payment schedule instead.
  • Skipping a written estimate. Read how to write a contractor estimate for what to include.
