How to Calculate Contractor Overhead (and Add It to Every Job)

Overhead is everything it costs to stay in business that no single job pays for on its own. If it is not inside your prices, it comes straight out of your profit.

Step 1: List a year of overhead

Use last year's bank statements or a realistic budget. An illustrative small trade business:

Truck payment, fuel, maintenance$9,600
Insurance (liability, vehicle)$6,000
Phone and software$2,400
Shop or office rent$12,000
Accounting and bookkeeping$3,000
Small tools and consumables$6,000
Marketing and listings$9,000
Total annual overhead$48,000

Leave out job materials and the labor on jobs: those are direct costs.

Step 2: Turn it into a rate

  • As a percentage of direct costs: $48,000 ÷ $200,000 of yearly materials and job labor = 24%.
  • Per billable hour (useful for service calls): $48,000 ÷ 1,600 billable hours = $30 per hour.

Pick one method and use it consistently. Recalculate at least once a year or when a big cost changes.

Step 3: Add it before you apply your margin

Direct cost of the job$2,000.00
Overhead: $2,000 × 24%$480.00
Total cost$2,480.00
Price at a 25% margin: $2,480 ÷ 0.75$3,306.67 (profit $826.67)

What happens if you skip it

Price the same job from direct cost only: $2,000 ÷ 0.75 = $2,666.67. Once the $480 of overhead is paid, real profit is $186.67, a 7% margin instead of the 25% you planned.
