How to Price Pest Control Services

Pest control is priced as a one-time treatment or a recurring plan, with a higher first visit and separate prices for termites, bed bugs and rodents. Here are the published rates, what moves a quote, and a worked first-year example.

One-time job or recurring plan

A one-time treatment solves a current problem. A recurring plan prevents the next one and gives you predictable income. Angi notes that ongoing services include treatment to keep ants, ticks, fleas and termites at bay, and that monthly visits cost less per treatment than a one-off. Quote both when you can; customers often take the plan once they see the per-visit price.

What moves the price

  • Type of pest. Angi says bed bugs and termites cost far more than ants or spiders, so quote them as separate jobs.
  • Severity. A light problem may take one visit; an established infestation needs repeat visits and monitoring.
  • Home and lot size. Bigger homes take more product and time; Angi's figures assume a 1,500 sq ft home.
  • Initial service. The first visit is the longest, with inspection and a heavier treatment, so price it above the recurring visit.
  • Contract and callbacks. Say whether free callbacks, warranty visits, interior service and a minimum term are included.
  • Exclusions. Termites, bed bugs and wildlife are usually not in a base plan. Write that down in the quote.

Compare the annual total

A customer comparing $125 per quarter with $50 per month is really comparing $500 with $600, and the first visit may add more. Show the first-year total and the renewal total so the number the customer remembers is the one you meant.

Check the price against your costs

Estimate the time per visit including drive time, multiply by your hourly cost, then add product, fuel, insurance and overhead. A route-based business should also price for density: several customers on the same street are worth more than the same number scattered across town. A worked first-year quote

One 1,500 sq ft home on a quarterly general pest plan, using example rates (yours will differ):

Initial service with inspection, 1 × $225$225.00
Three further quarterly visits, 3 × $125$375.00
First-year total$600.00

Renewal year: 4 × $125 = $500.00. Check it against the market: $225 is inside Angi's $150 to $300 initial visit, $125 is inside both Angi's $100 to $300 and HomeGuide's $100 to $270 quarterly range, and $500 sits inside Orkin's $500 to $1,000 and Terminix's $500 to $800 yearly plans. These figures are examples, not recommended prices.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing the first visit at the recurring rate.
  • Not saying which pests are excluded, then eating the cost of a termite or bed bug callout.
  • Quoting only per-visit prices, so the customer cannot see the annual cost.
  • Skipping a written estimate. Read how to write a contractor estimate for what to include.
