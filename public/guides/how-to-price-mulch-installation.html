How to Price Mulch Installation

Price mulch by the cubic yard, then check the result against crew pay and what the bed would cost per square foot. Here are the published rates, what moves a quote, and a worked example.

Typical prices for mulch installation

These are consumer-facing figures from 2026 cost guides, not rules. They show where the market sits so you can tell whether your price is far above or below it.

How much mulch the job needs

Cubic yards = bed area in square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324. One cubic yard covers about 162 sq ft at 2 inches and 108 sq ft at 3 inches (LawnStarter). Add a waste allowance for settling and uneven beds, then round up to the next quarter or half yard, because supply yards sell in those increments. A 2 cu ft bag is 1/13.5 of a cubic yard, so a bagged job needs 13.5 bags per yard. LawnStarter notes that bagged mulch can cost about 35% more than the same volume in bulk, so bags suit small top-ups only.

What moves the price

  • Mulch type. Shredded hardwood is the cheapest. Dyed, cedar and cypress cost more. Angi adds $10 to $30 per cubic yard for specialty mulch, and LawnStarter lists rubber mulch at $80 to $140 per cubic yard installed.
  • Delivery. Delivery is often a separate line, and LawnStarter says skipping it by self-hauling saves $70 to $140. Angi warns that small jobs may carry delivery surcharges or minimum fees.
  • Access and slope. Hand-spreading from a wheelbarrow takes longer where the truck cannot get close. Angi and HomeGuide both list tight access and slopes as reasons labor goes up.
  • Depth. Angi recommends 2 to 3 inches. Going from 2 to 3 inches adds half the volume of mulch.
  • Bed prep. Edging, weed barrier and removing old mulch are extra work. Put each on its own line.
  • Job size. Larger orders can earn a lower per-yard rate, while small jobs need a minimum charge.

Check the price against crew pay

Angi puts DIY spreading at about one hour per cubic yard including clean-up. Divide your installed price by the labor hours it takes and make sure the result covers wages, truck, fuel and taxes and still leaves profit. Set a minimum charge

A single small bed still costs you the trip, loading and clean-up. Set a minimum job charge from your hourly cost times the real time a small job takes, and show any adjustment as its own line.

A worked quote

One 30 × 10 ft bed (300 sq ft), 3 inches deep, using example rates (yours will differ):

Volume: 300 × 3 ÷ 324 = 2.78 cu yd; +5% waste = 2.92, rounded up to 3 cu yd
Mulch: 3 cu yd × $45$135.00
Spreading labor: 3 cu yd × $40$120.00
Total$255.00

Check it against the market: $255 ÷ 3 = $85 per cubic yard installed, inside HomeGuide's $50 to $150 and close to LawnStarter's $77 to $94 average. The $45 mulch price sits inside HomeGuide's $25 to $100, and the $40 labor rate inside Angi's $20 to $50. If you ordered in bags instead, 3 cu yd × 13.5 = 40.5, so 41 bags. The job clears a $150 minimum charge. These figures are examples, not recommended prices.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting by square foot without fixing the depth, so a 2 inch and a 3 inch job cost the same.
  • Forgetting a waste allowance and running short of mulch on the day.
  • Giving away delivery, edging, weed barrier or old mulch removal inside the mulch rate.
  • Pricing small jobs at the per-yard rate with no minimum charge.
  • Skipping a written estimate. Read how to write a contractor estimate for what to include.
