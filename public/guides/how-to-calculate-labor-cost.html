How to Calculate Labor Cost for a Contractor Bid

Multiplying hours by the wage is the most common way to underprice labor. An hour of labor costs more than the wage, and not every paid hour can be billed to a job.

Step 1: Start with the wage

Use the hourly wage you actually pay. Example: $25.00 per hour.

Step 2: Add labor burden

Burden is what you pay as an employer on top of the wage: the employer share of payroll taxes (Social Security and Medicare alone are 7.65% of wages), workers' compensation, unemployment insurance and any benefits. Add up last year's amounts and divide by total wages. In this example burden is 20%, so a paid hour costs $25.00 × 1.20 = $30.00.

Step 3: Divide by the billable share

Driving, loading, quoting, rain days and paid time off are paid but not billed. If 70% of paid hours end up on jobs, every billable hour has to carry the rest:

Paid hours per year (40 × 52)2,080
Billable hours at 70%1,456
Yearly wage + burden: 2,080 × $30.00$62,400
True cost per billable hour: $62,400 ÷ 1,456$42.86

The short version: $25 × 1.20 ÷ 0.70 = $42.86.

Put it in the bid

Estimated hours on the job16
Labor cost at the bare wage: 16 × $25$400.00
True labor cost: 16 × $42.86$685.71

Bidding from the wage would leave $285.71 of real cost out of this one job before any overhead or profit. Add materials and overhead to the true labor cost, then price from your target margin.
