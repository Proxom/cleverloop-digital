How to Price a Contractor Job

Most underpriced quotes have the same cause: the price is built by adding a percentage on top of cost (markup) when the owner was thinking in margin, and overhead is left out. Here is a simple four-step method that works for plumbers, electricians, painters, roofers, handymen and other home-service trades.

Step 1: Add up direct costs

Direct costs are what this job alone costs you: materials (including waste), labor hours times your cost per labor hour (wage plus taxes and insurance, see the labor burden calculator), and any subcontractor, dump or equipment rental fees.

Step 2: Add overhead

Overhead is the cost of running the business that no single job pays for: truck, insurance, phone, software, office time. A common shortcut is to express it as a percentage of direct costs. Your own number comes from last year's overhead divided by last year's direct costs.

Step 3: Price from margin, not markup

Price = total cost ÷ (1 − target margin). Adding 30% on top of cost gives only a 23% margin (why). If you want 30% of the price to be profit, divide by 0.70. The markup vs margin calculator converts between the two.

Step 4: Check discount, deposit and break-even

Before you send it, know your break-even price (total cost) and what happens if the client asks for 10% off. A discount comes straight out of profit: in the example below, 10% off drops the margin from 30% to about 22%.

Worked example

Materials$1,200.00
Labor: 24 hours × $35$840.00
Equipment rental$300.00
Direct costs$2,340.00
Overhead 12%$280.80
Total cost (break-even)$2,620.80
Price at 30% margin: $2,620.80 ÷ 0.70$3,744.00
Profit$1,123.20 (42.9% markup on cost, $46.80 per labor hour)
If the client gets 10% off ($3,369.60)profit $748.80, margin 22.2%
