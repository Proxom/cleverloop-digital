How to Price Lawn Mowing

Lawn mowing is priced per visit, by lawn size, with a higher price for the first cut of an overgrown yard and a small discount for recurring weekly service. Here are the published rates, what moves a quote, and a worked first-month example.

Three ways to price a lawn

  • Flat price per visit. The easiest for customers to understand. Work out the on-site time, multiply by your target hourly rate, and round to a clean number.
  • Per hour. Best for cleanups and overgrown lawns where the time is unpredictable. Angi says established pros target $50 to $90 per hour per operator in 2026.
  • Per acre or per square foot. Useful for large properties. Angi says square-foot pricing is uncommon except on very small lots, and Housecall Pro says there is no reliable per-square-foot benchmark. To check a flat price, divide it by the lawn's square footage.

What moves the price

  • Lawn size and shape. Open rectangles are quick; lots with beds, trees and tight corners take longer.
  • Terrain and access. LawnStarter says steep slopes, garden beds and hard-to-reach areas raise the cost.
  • Grass condition. An overgrown yard needs more passes and bagging, so quote the first cut separately.
  • Frequency. Weekly visits are shorter and cost slightly less per visit than biweekly or monthly cuts.
  • What is included. Trimming, edging and blowing are usually in the base price. Bagging, leaf cleanup and weed control are add-ons.
  • Travel and minimums. HomeGuide says most contractors have a 1-hour minimum, and Housecall Pro lists a travel fee for long-distance jobs.

Check the price against your costs

Estimate the on-site time plus drive time to the next stop, multiply by your hourly cost, then add fuel, equipment wear, insurance and overhead. Route density matters: five lawns on one street earn more per hour than five scattered across town. A worked first-month quote

One 1/4 acre lawn on weekly service, using example rates (yours will differ). On-site time: mowing 30 minutes, trimming and edging 15 minutes, blowing and cleanup 10 minutes, so 55 minutes. At a target of $70 per hour, 55 ÷ 60 × $70 = $64.17, rounded to $65.

Check it against the market: $65 sits inside Angi's $65 to $85 for a 1/4 acre lawn and HomeGuide's $30 to $85 per visit, $260 for four visits sits inside Angi's $65 to $100 per week, and $110 is inside the $70 to $150 range for an overgrown first cut. These figures are examples, not recommended prices.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing an overgrown first cut at the recurring rate.
  • Forgetting drive time, so a cheap-looking lawn loses money once the route is counted.
  • Not saying whether edging, bagging and cleanup are included.
  • Skipping a written estimate. Read how to write a contractor estimate for what to include.
