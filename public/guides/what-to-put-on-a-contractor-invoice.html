What to Put on a Contractor Invoice

An invoice is how you get paid. A clear one gets paid faster and settles arguments before they start. Here is what to put on it, how to set terms and late fees, and a worked example with a deposit credited.

What to put on a contractor invoice

  1. Your details. Business name, phone, e-mail, and license number if your state requires one on documents.
  2. Customer and job address. Who is billed and where the work was done.
  3. Invoice number. Unique and in order (for example 1042). It is how you and the customer find the invoice later.
  4. Invoice date and due date. Write the actual due date, not only "net 30".
  5. Itemized labor and materials. Quantity, unit price and amount per line, matching the accepted estimate. Added work should match a signed change order.
  6. Tax. Show it as its own line if it applies. Whether labor is taxable differs by state.
  7. Deposit or payments received. Subtract them so the customer sees the balance due.
  8. Total and balance due. One clear number at the bottom.
  9. How to pay. Check payee name, bank transfer details or a payment link, whatever you actually accept.
  10. Terms and late fee. One sentence, ideally agreed on the estimate before the work started.

A worked example

A bathroom faucet and drain repair, billed after the job, with a deposit paid at booking:

Materials: faucet $184.40, drain kit $62.00, fittings $66.00$312.40
Subtotal$872.40
Tax on materials: 8% × $312.40$24.99
Total$897.39
Deposit received−$250.00
Balance due$647.39

The numbers are illustrative; tax rules for labor and materials vary by state.

Payment terms: pick one and write the date

TermMeaningGood for
Due on receiptPay as soon as the invoice is receivedSmall jobs, repairs, one-day work
Net 15Pay within 15 days of the invoice dateMid-size residential jobs
Net 30Pay within 30 days of the invoice dateLarger jobs, commercial customers
MilestonesInvoice at set stages (for example deposit, rough-in, completion)Long or expensive projects; plan them with the payment schedule calculator

Late fees

If you charge one, agree it in writing before the work, usually on the accepted estimate, and repeat it on the invoice, for example: "Balance due by June 15. A late fee of 1.5% per month applies to overdue balances." Rules on maximum rates and required wording differ by state, so check yours. See the late payment fee calculator to see what a fee comes to.

Common mistakes

  • Sending an invoice that does not match the estimate, with no change order for the difference.
  • Forgetting to credit the deposit, then having to send a corrected invoice.
  • Writing "net 30" without the date, so nobody knows when it is late.
  • Leaving out how to pay, which adds days to every payment.
  • Waiting weeks to invoice. Send it the day the work is done or the milestone is reached.
