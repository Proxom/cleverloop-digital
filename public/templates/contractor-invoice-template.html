What is in the template

  • Header for your business name, address, phone, email and license number
  • Invoice number, invoice date, due date and the estimate number it relates to
  • Bill-to customer, job address and a line for the work completed
  • 12 line-item rows (quantity × unit price), with room for signed change orders
  • Subtotal, sales tax on the taxable amount, invoice total, deposit and other payments received, and the balance due, all automatic
  • Editable payment instructions; prints on one page

How to invoice a finished job

  1. Start from the signed estimate. Copy the line items from your estimate so the invoice matches what the customer agreed to.
  2. Add every signed change order. Extra work goes on its own line with the price the customer signed. See how to price a change order.
  3. Apply sales tax only where it applies. Enter the taxable amount and your rate; rules differ by state and by trade. The job sales tax calculator shows the math.
  4. Subtract what you already received. Enter the deposit and any progress payments; the template shows the balance due.
  5. Set a clear due date and how to pay. If you accept cards, check what the fee costs you with the invoice processing fee calculator.
  6. Follow up on time. If your contract allows a late fee, the late payment fee calculator estimates it.

Frequently asked questions

What should a contractor invoice include?

Your business name, contact details and license number if your state requires one; the word Invoice, an invoice number, invoice date and due date; the customer and job address; the work completed as line items, including any signed change orders; subtotal, sales tax, total, payments already received and the balance due; and how to pay.

How do I show the deposit on the final invoice?

List the full job as line items so the invoice total matches the agreed price plus any change orders, then subtract the deposit already received on its own line. The template does this for you and shows the balance due.

What payment terms should a contractor use?

Common terms are due on receipt or net 15 / net 30 (payment due 15 or 30 days after the invoice date). Use what your contract says, put the due date on the invoice, and check your state's rules before adding late fees.
